ONE-ROUND-LAG EV PROBE: POSITIVE. Instrumented dspark-run to emit full per-round proposal confidence vectors (proposal_confidences field, lmbrrr HEAD), ran the 18-q calibration split, and simmed the lagged scheduler offline (python replica of schedule_prefix_width + per-position Platt STS + wave1 cost model; replica cross-checked exactly against the 282 recorded width choices). RESULTS: width match rate 68.8% (choices are bimodal w in {0,3} under current economics); regret of using round r-1's vector, measured by the scheduler's own objective under round r's fresh beliefs: mean 1.98%, median 0.00% — a small minority of 0<->3 flips carry ~10-47% single-round regret, everything else is free. Against the drain saving (~1-2ms OS wait on a ~10ms drafted round = 10-20% of round wall), lag is clearly EV-positive on paper. Caveats for the implementation A/B: (a) the sim pairs consecutive DRAFTED rounds — interleaved PLD/skip rounds make vectors staler in wall time but the pairing matches what the implementation would use; (b) hysteresis feedback (consecutive_zero_widths) is outside the sim — the rotated suite A/B decides. DESIGN settled by the probe: propose() keeps tokens+confidences on device; width for round r from round r-1's vector; verify chunk assembled on-device (cat anchor + token chain, narrow to width); tokens+confidences read back WITH the verify targets (2 drains -> 1); first drafted round after a gap falls back to the synchronous path (no lagged vector).
